Students use CGPA, SGPA and GPA almost interchangeably — and then lose marks on application forms by putting the wrong number in the wrong field. They are three different measurements. Here is each one, precisely.

## SGPA: One Semester

**SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average)** measures your performance in a *single semester*. Each subject's grade points are multiplied by its credits, summed, and divided by the semester's total credits:

```
SGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credits in semester) ÷ Σ(Credits in semester)
```

Your university prints an SGPA on every semester marksheet. A student scoring O (10) in four subjects and A (8) in two, with equal credits, earns an SGPA of about 9.3 for that semester alone.

## CGPA: The Whole Programme

**CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average)** aggregates every completed semester, weighted by credits:

```
CGPA = Σ(SGPA × Semester Credits) ÷ Σ(Total Credits)
```

After four semesters, your CGPA is the credit-weighted blend of all four SGPAs. This is why our [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) asks for credits — a plain average of your SGPAs is wrong whenever semesters carry different credit loads, which most do (project semesters typically carry more).

**The relationship in one line:** SGPA is a chapter; CGPA is the whole book so far.

## GPA: The 4.0 Scale

**GPA**, in the Indian context, usually refers to the **4.0 scale** used by the US, Canada and many other countries. It is not a different *timeframe* — it is a different *scale*. Your whole-programme average can be expressed as either:

- 8.4 on a 10-point scale (Indian style), or
- roughly 3.36 on a 4.0 scale (US style), using the linear conversion (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4.

Confusingly, some Indian institutions also say "GPA" for their per-semester average. When a form says "GPA", check the scale it expects — that tells you which number to enter. Our [CGPA to GPA converter](/cgpa-to-gpa) handles both directions.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| | SGPA | CGPA | GPA (4.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | One semester | All semesters so far | All semesters (different scale) |
| **Scale** | 0–10 (usually) | 0–10 (usually) | 0.0–4.0 |
| **Printed on** | Semester marksheet | Consolidated marksheet | Not used in India |
| **Used for** | Tracking semester progress | Placements, Indian applications | US/Canada applications |
| **Weighted by** | Subject credits | Semester credits | Course credits |

## Common Mistakes

- **Writing SGPA where CGPA is asked.** Your best semester's SGPA is not your CGPA — verification teams catch this instantly, and it reads as misrepresentation.
- **Averaging SGPAs without credits.** (7.8×22 + 8.6×24) ÷ 46 is not the same as (7.8 + 8.6) ÷ 2. Always weight.
- **Converting CGPA to a percentage with the wrong formula** — the ×9.5 rule is not universal. Check the [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) for your university.
- **Self-converting to 4.0 for US applications** that actually want your original scale. Most prefer "8.4/10.0" plus an official WES-style evaluation.

## The Practical Workflow

1. Each semester, note your SGPA from the marksheet.
2. Project your running CGPA with the [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa).
3. For Indian job and exam forms, convert CGPA to percentage with your university's formula (start at the [main calculator](/)).
4. For foreign applications, keep the original CGPA and order an official evaluation; use the [CGPA to GPA tool](/cgpa-to-gpa) only for self-assessment.
