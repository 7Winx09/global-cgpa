It's the most tempting decimal in academics: your transcript says **7.96**, the form asks for your CGPA, and "8.0" would look so much better. Is rounding legitimate? The answer has more nuance — and more consequences — than students expect.

## The General Rule

**Your CGPA is the number printed on your marksheet — to the exact decimals shown.** Universities compute the credit-weighted average and typically round to **two decimal places** on official documents. That printed figure is your CGPA for every official purpose:

- Application forms: write 7.96, not 8.
- Resumes: 7.96/10 (or 7.96/10 with your converted percentage).
- Verification: your figure must match the transcript digit-for-digit.

Rounding 7.96 to 8.0 on a form is not "presenting" — it is stating a number your university never issued. At best it's corrected silently; at worst (offer stage, background check) it's treated as misrepresentation.

## Where Rounding *Is* Acceptable

There are exactly two defensible rounding scenarios:

1. **The university itself rounds.** Some ordinances specify that the final CGPA is rounded (often to one decimal, or upward at .5+) *for classification purposes*. If your ordinance says so, the university applies it — you don't.
2. **The receiving organisation rounds.** Some employers and portals round percentages to whole numbers for eligibility checks (70.78% → 71%). That's their rule, applied to the exact figure you provide. You still supply 70.78%.

Everything else — rounding up on a resume, rounding to clear a cut-off, rounding "because it's basically the same" — is you altering an official record.

## The Percentage Side: Keep the Decimals

When converting, the same discipline applies. If your CGPA is 7.45 under the AKTU formula:

```
(7.45 − 0.75) × 10 = 67.00%   ✓ correct
"about 67.5%"                  ✗ wrong
"roughly 68%"                  ✗ wrong
```

Every formula on our [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) is linear and exact — there is no estimation step. Write two decimals (67.00%), and if a form demands whole numbers, let *their* rounding rule do the rounding.

## Why This Gets People in Trouble

- **Placement verification:** companies collect transcripts and recompute your percentage with your university's formula. A resume claiming 8.0 against a 7.96 transcript reads as dishonesty over a difference of 0.04 — and the dishonesty, not the number, is disqualifying.
- **Government document verification:** forms are signed declarations. The figure must be derivable from your documents exactly.
- **Foreign credential evaluation:** evaluators report what transcripts say; a mismatch between your application and the evaluation triggers flags.

## Legitimate Ways to Present a Better Number

You never need to round, because honest alternatives exist:

- **Show both figures:** "CGPA 7.96/10 (75.6%)" — the percentage often looks stronger than the CGPA suggests, depending on the formula.
- **Show trajectory:** "Final Year CGPA: 8.4/10" as a secondary line, with the aggregate stated honestly alongside.
- **Improve the real number.** If credits remain, every future semester still moves your CGPA — model the effect with our [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) and read the [improvement guide](/guides/how-to-improve-cgpa).
- **Lead with strengths elsewhere:** projects, internships, and test scores outperform 0.04 CGPA in every hiring decision ever made.

## The One-Sentence Policy

Report the transcript number exactly, convert with your university's exact formula, keep two decimals end to end — and spend your creativity on the parts of your profile that honest presentation can make look great.
