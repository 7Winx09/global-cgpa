"First Class with Distinction" is printed on degree certificates, quoted in interviews, and required by some employers — but the thresholds behind the phrase change from university to university. Here's what the divisions mean, where the lines fall at major universities, and how to check your own.

## The Standard Division Structure

Most Indian universities classify final degrees into four bands:

| Division | Typical percentage | Typical CGPA (10-point) |
|---|---|---|
| First Class with Distinction | 70–75% and above | 7.0 – 8.5+ (varies sharply) |
| First Class | 60% and above | 6.0+ |
| Second Class | 50% and above | 5.0+ |
| Pass Class | 40–45% and above | 4.0–4.5 |

The percentages are broadly consistent; the CGPAs that produce them are not, because each university converts CGPA to percentage with its own formula. A 6.5 CGPA is 65% at VTU but only 57.5% at SPPU — First Class at one, Second Class at the other.

## Thresholds at Major Universities

These are the commonly applied boundaries; always confirm against your own ordinance, as conditions (like first-attempt passes) often apply:

| University | Distinction | First Class | Second Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| VTU (Karnataka) | ~7.0 CGPA (70%+) | 6.0 CGPA (60%+) | 5.0 CGPA (50%+) |
| Anna University (Tamil Nadu) | ~8.5 CGPA, no arrears | 6.5 CGPA (65%+) | 5.0 CGPA (50%+) |
| AKTU (Uttar Pradesh) | 8.25 CGPA (75%+), first-attempt pass | 6.75 CGPA (60%+) | ~5.25 CGPA |
| GTU (Gujarat) | 7.1 CPI (≈66%) | 6.5 CPI (60%) | 5.5 CPI (50%) |
| KTU (Kerala) | ~8.5 CGPA (Honours) | 6.5 CGPA (65%+) | ~5.5 CGPA |
| SPPU (Maharashtra) | ~7.75+ CGPA | ~6.75+ CGPA | ~5.5+ CGPA |

Three patterns are worth noticing:

- **Anna University and KTU demand the most for Distinction** (8.5+), partly because their ×10 conversion makes 85% the natural line.
- **AKTU adds a conduct condition** — Distinction usually requires clearing every subject in the first attempt, so a cleared backlog can cost you the tag even with an 8.5 CGPA.
- **GTU's Distinction line (7.1) looks low but converts to just 66%** under its own formula, and GTU does *not* require a first-attempt pass for it.

## Why the Division on Your Certificate Matters

1. **Government forms ask for it.** Many state and central recruitment applications have a "Class obtained" field, and some reserve eligibility relaxations by division.
2. **Some employers filter on it.** "First Class throughout" (10th, 12th and degree) appears in certain PSU and banking notifications as an eligibility criterion.
3. **It survives on your degree certificate permanently.** Unlike a CGPA you might improve, the division is awarded once, at graduation, based on final figures.

## How to Check Your Division

1. Find your final CGPA on your consolidated marksheet (or project it with the [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa)).
2. Convert it using your university's official formula — the [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) lists all of them, and the university pages ([SPPU](/sppu-cgpa-to-percentage), [VTU](/vtu-cgpa-to-percentage), [Anna](/anna-university-cgpa-to-percentage), [AKTU](/aktu-cgpa-to-percentage), [GTU](/gtu-cgpa-to-percentage), [KTU](/ktu-cgpa-to-percentage), [Mumbai](/mumbai-university-cgpa-to-percentage)) explain each.
3. Match the percentage against your ordinance's classification table — usually in the "Award of Degree" or "Classification" section.
4. If you're within 0.1 CGPA of a boundary, check the conditions (arrears, first-attempt rules) before claiming the higher division on any form.

## A Note on "Equivalent Percentage" Claims

Students sometimes compute their division using the generic ×9.5 formula when their university uses a different one — and claim First Class based on the wrong number. Verification teams use your university's ordinance, not the CBSE rule. Compute it right the first time; the [percentage to CGPA reverse converter](/percentage-to-cgpa) is useful for checking eligibility thresholds from the percentage side.
