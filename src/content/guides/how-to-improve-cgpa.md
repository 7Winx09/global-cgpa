"Score better" is not a plan. Improving your CGPA is a resource-allocation problem: limited effort, unequal payoffs, and a formula that tells you exactly where the leverage is. This guide turns the CGPA maths into decisions you can act on this week.

## First, Know Your Leverage

Your CGPA is a credit-weighted average:

```
CGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credits) ÷ Σ(Total Credits)
```

Three levers follow, in order of power:

1. **Grade boundaries in high-credit subjects.** Moving a 4-credit subject from B+ (7) to A (8) adds 4 weighted points; the same jump in a 2-credit lab adds 2. Same effort, double effect.
2. **Band-crossing beats mark-chasing.** Grade points are awarded in bands — 74 and 70 both earn an A (8 points), but 79→80 jumps to 9. Effort spent crossing a band edge pays a full point; effort inside a band pays zero.
3. **Remaining credits.** Every semester not yet completed is recovery capacity. The earlier you start, the more of your CGPA is still movable.

## Step 1: Audit Your Position (15 Minutes)

List your completed semesters: SGPA and credits each. Feed them into the [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) for your exact current CGPA and total credits. Then compute your ceiling:

```
Ceiling = (current points + 10 × remaining credits) ÷ (total programme credits)
```

A 7.2 CGPA with 80 of 160 credits done has a ceiling of (576 + 800) ÷ 160 = **8.6**. A 7.2 with 130 credits done tops out at (936 + 300) ÷ 160 = **7.73**. Your ceiling determines whether your goal is "distinction" or "damage control" — both are respectable strategies, but they're different.

## Step 2: Target the Right Subjects This Semester

For each current subject, estimate two things: **credits** and **your likely marks distance to the next grade band**. Then rank by:

```
Impact = Credits × (1 if you're within ~5 marks of a band edge)
```

Typical outcome of this ranking:

- **Top priority:** 4-credit core subjects where you're hovering at 68–79 (one exam away from B+→A or A→A+).
- **Cheap wins:** 1–2 credit labs and seminars — near-certain A/A+ grades for modest effort. Never leave these at B.
- **Containment:** subjects you're genuinely failing — the goal shifts from band-jumping to passing, because a 0-point subject costs double (its own credits × 10 in lost points).

## Step 3: Exploit the Structures Universities Give You

- **Electives are strategic weapons.** Where your scheme offers choices, pick electives aligned with your strengths or known scoring patterns (ask seniors about grading leniency — it varies by instructor, legally and openly).
- **Continuous assessment is bankable.** Internals, assignments and lab records are the marks you fully control. Maximising internals typically adds 3–5 marks per subject before exams even begin — often the exact band-edge margin you need.
- **The final-year project is the biggest single lever.** At 8–12 credits across two semesters, moving your project from B to O can shift your final CGPA by 0.15–0.3 on its own. Start early; treat your guide's feedback as exam syllabus.
- **Attend the improvement/supplementary chances your ordinance offers.** Some universities allow grade improvement exams for passed subjects — check the rules and deadlines in your regulation document.

## Step 4: The Exam-Week Band-Edge Strategy

In the last week before exams, allocate revision by *marginal marks*:

1. Compute each subject's band-edge gap (marks needed for the next grade).
2. Rank subjects by gap ÷ your confidence.
3. Spend the final days on the smallest gaps in the highest-credit subjects — not on perfecting subjects already safely in-band.
4. In the exam itself, secure the band-edge marks first (the questions you know target 60–80% of marks), then attempt the stretch material.

## Step 5: Track and Recompute Every Semester

After each result: recompute your CGPA, recompute your ceiling, and re-rank next semester's subjects. The plan is a loop, not a one-time fix. When forms ask for your running percentage, convert with your own university's rule — the [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) covers every major formula.

## What Not to Do

- **Don't chase 95+ marks in banded subjects.** Inside a band, extra marks buy nothing. The system rewards boundary-crossing, not perfection.
- **Don't ignore 1-credit subjects.** They're the cheapest grade points on campus.
- **Don't let a backlog age.** Every attempt cycle it survives costs you points and, at some universities, Distinction eligibility (see [backlog recovery](/guides/does-backlog-affect-cgpa)).
- **Don't round or inflate on documents while "working on it".** Report exact figures now; improve the real number instead (see [rounding rules](/guides/cgpa-rounding-off-rules)).

## The Realistic Expectation

With two strong semesters remaining, moving from 7.0 to 7.6–7.8 is very achievable; 7.0 to 8.5 is not. Set your target from the ceiling math in Step 1, execute the credit-weighted priorities, and remember what the number is for: it buys you at-bats in placements and admissions. Beyond a solid threshold, your projects and skills take over — which is exactly where your time should go once the CGPA maths says you're safe.
