If you are in an Indian college or university, almost every important academic conversation eventually arrives at four letters: **CGPA**. It appears on your marksheet, decides placement eligibility, and shows up in every higher-studies application. Yet many students reach their final year without a precise understanding of what it actually measures. This guide fixes that.

## CGPA: Full Form and Definition

CGPA stands for **Cumulative Grade Point Average**. It is a single number, usually on a scale of 0 to 10, that summarises your overall academic performance across every subject and semester completed so far in your programme.

Breaking the name down explains the mechanism:

- **Grade Point** — every subject you take is graded (O, A+, A, B+ and so on), and each grade maps to a number between 0 and 10 called a grade point.
- **Average** — your grade points are averaged, not by simple mean, but weighted by the **credits** each subject carries.
- **Cumulative** — the average runs across *all* semesters of your programme, not just one. (The single-semester version is called SGPA — Semester Grade Point Average.)

## How Universities Calculate CGPA

The universal formula used by Indian universities is:

```
CGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credits) ÷ Σ(Total Credits)
```

**Worked example.** Suppose you completed four subjects in a programme so far:

| Subject | Credits | Grade | Grade Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 4 | A+ | 9 |
| Data Structures | 4 | O | 10 |
| Electronics | 3 | A | 8 |
| Workshop | 2 | B+ | 7 |

Weighted points = (9×4) + (10×4) + (8×3) + (7×2) = 36 + 40 + 24 + 14 = 114. Total credits = 13. CGPA = 114 ÷ 13 = **8.77**.

Two things follow directly from this formula, and both matter strategically:

1. **Credits are leverage.** A 4-credit subject moves your CGPA twice as much as a 2-credit subject. Scoring well in high-credit core subjects is the efficient way to raise your average.
2. **Early semesters anchor you.** Every new semester's grades are averaged into a growing pool of completed credits, so a weak first year takes increasingly strong performance to repair. (See our guide on [recovering from backlogs](/guides/does-backlog-affect-cgpa).)

## What Is a Good CGPA?

On the standard 10-point scale used by most Indian universities:

| CGPA | Rough Percentage (×9.5) | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| 9.0 – 10.0 | 85.5 – 95% | Top of the batch; distinction territory |
| 8.0 – 8.9 | 76 – 84.5% | Very good; competitive for everything |
| 7.0 – 7.9 | 66.5 – 75% | Good; clears most placement cut-offs |
| 6.0 – 6.9 | 57 – 66.5% | Average; meets basic eligibility bars |
| Below 6.0 | Under 57% | Often below placement and PSU minimums |

Note that the percentage column uses the generic CBSE multiplier. Your university may convert differently — SPPU, Mumbai University, AKTU and GTU all have their own formulas, which you can check in our [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart).

## Why CGPA Exists at All

Before credit-based grading, Indian universities reported raw percentages. The switch to CGPA (driven by UGC's Choice Based Credit System framework) happened for practical reasons:

- **Comparability.** Percentages vary wildly with exam difficulty; grade points smooth out year-to-year differences.
- **Flexibility.** Credit systems let students pick electives, and CGPA aggregates mixed course loads fairly.
- **International alignment.** Most of the world grades on point scales (the US uses 4.0), making transcripts easier to evaluate across borders.

## What CGPA Does *Not* Tell You

CGPA is useful but incomplete. It does not capture the difficulty of your courses, your project or internship work, or your skills — which is why recruiters pair CGPA cut-offs with tests and interviews. It is a filter, not a verdict. A 7.5 CGPA with strong projects routinely beats a 9.0 with none.
