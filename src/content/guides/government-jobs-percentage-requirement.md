Government job notifications are written in percentages, but Indian universities issue marksheets in CGPA. Every year, applicants lose opportunities to that translation gap — by converting with the wrong formula, rounding incorrectly, or misreading a classification requirement. This guide covers how major government recruitments treat your grades and how to fill the forms correctly.

## The Core Rule: Convert With Your University's Formula

Government verifying authorities do not use the CBSE ×9.5 default when your university prescribes its own conversion. If a form asks for "percentage of marks", the defensible figure is the one derived from your university's official ordinance — which is exactly what our [university-specific calculators](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) apply. When in doubt, obtain a conversion certificate from your university (see our guide on [conversion certificates](/guides/cgpa-to-percentage-certificate)); it settles every future dispute in one document.

## SSC Exams (CGL, CHSL, MTS)

- **Tier-I and Tier-II scores are exam-based** — your college marks do not factor into merit.
- **Educational qualification is the only academic filter**, typically "Bachelor's degree in any discipline" — no minimum percentage for most CGL posts.
- Where a specific post does list a percentage minimum, your converted percentage plus your university's formula reference is what the verification team checks.

## Banking (IBPS, SBI, RRB)

- Most officer and clerk posts require only **graduation in any discipline** — no percentage cut-off.
- A few specialised posts (agriculture officers, IT officers) require a relevant degree, again usually without a percentage bar.
- Your CGPA matters here only in the background-verification sense: your degree must be genuine and complete before joining.

## PSU Recruitment (ONGC, NTPC, BHEL, GATE-based)

PSUs are where academic marks matter most:

- **Most engineering PSUs recruit through GATE**, but many add an eligibility bar of **60–65% or equivalent CGPA** in the engineering degree.
- **Apprentice and graduate-trainee intakes** frequently state "minimum 60% aggregate or 6.5 CGPA" — and expect the CGPA converted by your university's rule.
- **Classification requirements occasionally appear** — "First Class degree" in some notifications, which maps to your university's own First Class threshold (see [division rules](/guides/first-class-second-class-division)).

## State and Central University Teaching & Other Roles

- Assistant Professor and teacher-recruitment notifications typically ask for **55% at the postgraduate level** (with relaxations for reserved categories), converted per the awarding university's formula.
- UGC-NET qualification itself is score-based, but eligibility percentage checks happen at verification.

## Filling the Form Correctly: A Checklist

1. **Find the exact wording.** "Percentage of marks", "CGPA", or "either" — each expects a different entry.
2. **Convert once, correctly.** Use your university's formula ([SPPU](/sppu-cgpa-to-percentage), [VTU](/vtu-cgpa-to-percentage), [Anna](/anna-university-cgpa-to-percentage), [AKTU](/aktu-cgpa-to-percentage), [GTU](/gtu-cgpa-to-percentage), [KTU](/ktu-cgpa-to-percentage), [Mumbai](/mumbai-university-cgpa-to-percentage) or the generic [calculator](/)).
3. **Keep two decimals.** If your CGPA is 7.45, the percentage is 70.78% (CBSE) — not 70% and not 71%. Rounding mismatches are a common verification flag.
4. **Match your documents.** Whatever you write must be reproducible from your marksheet plus your university's ordinance. If your university has issued a conversion certificate, use that exact figure.
5. **Keep proof of the formula.** A printout or link to your university's ordinance clause is your defence if a verifier questions the number.

## Common Mistakes That Cost Interviews

- Using ×9.5 when your university uses (CGPA × 10) − 7.5 or (7.1 × CGPA) + 11 — producing a figure your own certificate contradicts.
- Writing SGPA (one semester) where cumulative CGPA was asked.
- Claiming "First Class" without checking your university's Distinction/First Class conditions, especially first-attempt rules at AKTU and similar ordinances.
- Leaving the CGPA field blank when the form allows either — a filled, correct percentage always beats an empty field.

The system is bureaucratic but not hostile: it simply demands that the number on your form be derivable from your documents. Get the conversion right once, save it, and every government form for the rest of your career takes thirty seconds.
