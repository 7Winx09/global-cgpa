Few academic events cause as much panic as a backlog. Beyond the immediate stress, the questions multiply: how much will my CGPA drop? Can I recover it? Will it show forever? Here are the mechanics, the actual numbers, and the recovery plan.

## The Immediate Damage: A Zero in the Weighted Average

A failed subject earns **0 grade points but keeps its credits** in the CGPA formula:

```
CGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credits) ÷ Σ(Total Credits)
```

**Worked example:** after three semesters with a CGPA of 8.0 across 66 credits, you fail one 4-credit subject in semester four while earning 8.5 on the remaining 20 credits:

- Before: 8.0 × 66 = 528 weighted points
- New points: 8.5 × 20 = 170; the failed subject adds 0 × 4 = 0
- New CGPA = (528 + 170) ÷ 90 = **7.76**

One 4-credit backlog cost about **0.24 CGPA** in a single semester. The damage scales with the subject's credits — failing a 4-credit core hurts twice as much as a 2-credit lab.

## What Happens When You Clear It

In nearly all universities, when you pass the re-examination, **the new grade replaces the zero** in the CGPA calculation. The subject's credits then earn their grade points as if counted from a normal semester. In the example above, clearing the backlog with an A (8 points) adds 32 points, restoring your average toward where it would have been.

What usually does *not* disappear:

- **Transcript notation.** Many universities mark the re-appearance ("*", "R", or a second entry for the subject). The CGPA recovers; the record remains visible.
- **Time.** You carry the subject alongside new semesters until cleared.
- **Distinction eligibility at some universities.** AKTU and several ordinances require clearing every subject in the first attempt for Distinction, regardless of final CGPA. GTU, notably, does not.

## The Recovery Math

The encouraging part: because CGPA is credit-weighted, **remaining credits are your recovery fuel**. The formula for your achievable CGPA:

```
Best possible CGPA = (current weighted points + 10 × remaining credits) ÷ (completed + remaining credits)
```

**Example:** 7.0 CGPA after 80 credits of a 160-credit degree:

- Current points: 560
- Perfect finish: 560 + (10 × 80) = 1,360 ÷ 160 = **8.5 achievable**

At 120 credits completed, the same 7.0 student can only reach (840 + 400) ÷ 160 = **7.75**. The lesson: recovery power halves with every year that passes. Model your own case with the [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) — enter your completed semesters plus projected perfect ones.

## The Practical Recovery Plan

1. **Clear the backlog at the earliest attempt.** Every attempt cycle it survives, it suppresses your average and delays Distinction eligibility.
2. **Prioritise high-credit backlogs.** If clearing multiple, the 4-credit subject first — same effort, double CGPA effect.
3. **Over-shoot the retake.** An A on the retake doesn't just remove the zero; it actively adds points. Treat the re-exam as an opportunity to *gain*, not repair.
4. **Protect current semesters.** The worst outcome is a backlog cycle that drags current-semester grades down too. One bad semester is recoverable; two consecutive ones are a trend.
5. **Check your ordinance's notation and class rules.** Know whether your transcript marks re-appearances and whether your target class (Distinction/First) has first-attempt conditions — see [division rules explained](/guides/first-class-second-class-division).

## Backlogs and Placements: The Honest Picture

- **Cleared backlogs** rarely block offers; many companies only require "no active backlogs" at joining.
- **Active backlogs** disqualify you from most campus processes until cleared.
- **Some companies count attempts** ("maximum 2 years gap/backlogs" clauses in service agreements) — read the eligibility fine print.
- For **PSUs and government jobs**, classification conditions (first-attempt Distinction rules) matter more than the backlog itself once cleared.

## Bottom Line

A backlog is a mathematically defined problem with a mathematically defined solution: clear it fast, score high on the retake, and keep future semesters strong. The CGPA recovers; the strategy is entirely about *when* and *by how much*.
