If you studied under a percentage system in school, your first college marksheet can look like a code book: O, A+, B, grade points, credits, SGPA. This guide decodes the standard 10-point grading system used by most Indian technical universities.

## The 10-Point Scale and Letter Grades

Most Indian universities map raw marks to letter grades, and each letter carries a fixed **grade point**. The most common pattern:

| Letter Grade | Grade Point | Typical Marks Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| O | 10 | 90–100% | Outstanding |
| A+ | 9 | 80–89% | Excellent |
| A | 8 | 70–79% | Very Good |
| B+ | 7 | 60–69% | Good |
| B | 6 | 50–59% | Average |
| C | 5 | 40–49% | Pass |
| F / U | 0 | Below pass | Fail / Reappear |

The bands are *not* percentages of your final result — they are translation tables. Scoring an 84 does not give you "84%"; it gives you an A+ and 9 grade points, and your final CGPA is built from those points.

## From Grades to CGPA in Three Steps

1. **Each subject earns a grade point.** Your 84 in Data Structures becomes 9 points.
2. **Points are credit-weighted and averaged per semester.** A 4-credit subject counts twice as much as a 2-credit lab. The result is your SGPA.
3. **Semi­sters combine into CGPA**, again weighted by credits — this is what our [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) automates.

Because of the banding, the system has a useful quirk: within a band, extra marks earn nothing. An 80 and an 89 both produce an A+ (9 points). But crossing a band edge — 79 to 80 — jumps you a full grade point, which is why exam-time strategy should focus on the subjects where you are near a boundary.

## Variations Between Universities

The framework is standard; the details are not:

- **Band edges differ.** Some universities start the O band at 91 or 92 rather than 90.
- **Pass grades differ.** Anna University's lowest pass grade is C (5 points); GTU's is DD (4 points) with a pass mark of 35; AKTU adds an E grade (4 points) for a conditional pass.
- **Terminology differs.** GTU uses SPI/CPI instead of SGPA/CGPA. Some autonomous colleges publish their own tables entirely.
- **Conversion rules differ.** Each university publishes its own CGPA-to-percentage formula — see our [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) for all of them side by side.

Always read the grade table printed in your own university's regulation document or marksheet legend; it overrides any general guide, including this one.

## Pass Marks and Backlogs

A subject with an F or U grade earns **zero** grade points but still adds to your credit load in the CGPA formula — which is exactly why a single failed subject drags a CGPA down sharply. When you clear the back paper, the new grade typically *replaces* the zero in the calculation, recovering your average, though your transcript may note the re-appearance. We cover the mechanics in [do backlogs affect your CGPA](/guides/does-backlog-affect-cgpa).

## Why Universities Moved to This System

The UGC's Choice Based Credit System framework pushed Indian universities onto point scales for three reasons: grade points smooth out year-to-year exam difficulty, credit weighting makes mixed course loads comparable, and point scales translate cleanly to international systems like the US 4.0 GPA. Whether it achieves all that is debatable — but for now, the 10-point system is the language of Indian higher education, and reading it fluently is a genuine advantage in placements and applications.
