Studying abroad without funding is mostly a rich family's game; with funding, it's a merit game — and your CGPA is the first filter every scholarship applies. Here are the realistic thresholds for the major programmes Indian students target, and what to do if your CGPA is below them.

## The General Thresholds

| Funding type | Realistic CGPA target (10-point) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fully funded PhD / TA-RA assistantships (US) | 8.0+ | Research fit and publications can bend this |
| Competitive MS scholarships (US) | 8.5+ | Departmental awards, diversity scholarships |
| DAAD (Germany) | 8.0+ (German grade equivalent) | Converted via Bavarian formula; work experience weighs heavily for EPOS programmes |
| Chevening (UK) | 7.5+ | Leadership story matters more than marks |
| Commonwealth Master's | 7.5–8.0+ | Development-impact narrative critical |
| Australia Awards | 7.0–7.5+ | Work experience and home-country impact |
| University tuition waivers (Canada, Europe) | 7.5–8.5+ | Varies enormously by department |

Two structural notes: PhD funding is more accessible than MS funding (you provide labour worth paying for), and **European programmes weight work experience and motivation far more than American ones**, which softens CGPA requirements in practice.

## What Scholarship Committees Actually Evaluate

Your CGPA opens the folder; it rarely wins the money by itself. Selection typically weighs:

1. **Academic record (CGPA + institution reputation)** — the filter, roughly 20–30% of the decision.
2. **Research or professional output** — publications, projects, patents, product launches.
3. **Statement of purpose / motivation** — for scholarship programmes specifically, *impact*: what you'll do with the degree for your field or country.
4. **Recommendations** — strong, specific letters can move a borderline file more than 0.2 CGPA.
5. **Standardised tests** — a strong GRE/GATE/IELTS offsets a modest CGPA by demonstrating current capability.
6. **Work experience** — decisive for DAAD EPOS, Chevening, Commonwealth and MBA funding.

## If Your CGPA Is Below the Target

A 7.2 CGPA does not end the plan; it changes the route:

- **Add a proof-of-current-ability:** a high GRE quant score, a GATE score, published research, or a strong GitHub/portfolio. Committees read a strong recent score as "the CGPA understates them".
- **Target experience-first programmes:** DAAD EPOS schemes, Chevening and Commonwealth explicitly favour 2+ years of work impact over academic polish.
- **Consider a domestic master's first:** a 9.0+ M.Tech/GATE-funded record resets your academic history entirely for a funded PhD abroad.
- **Apply where your profile is above median, not at minimum.** Scholarships are won against the applicant pool, not the published floor.
- **Get your conversions exact.** Some committees see your converted percentage before anything else; a wrong conversion (using ×9.5 when your university uses a different rule) can understate you by 3–7 points. Our [university conversion pages](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) prevent that.

## Timeline That Actually Works

- **12–18 months before:** lock your target list; note each programme's CGPA/evaluation requirements; start the [conversion certificate](/guides/cgpa-to-percentage-certificate) process if needed.
- **9–12 months:** standardised tests; draft SOPs around impact, not marks; line up recommenders who know your work specifically.
- **6–9 months:** applications open (Chevening opens ~August for the following year; DAAD deadlines cluster September–October for the next winter intake; US assistantship rounds peak December–January).
- **Ongoing:** every 0.1 CGPA still counts while you're studying — final-semester grades arrive before some funding decisions.

## The Honest Bottom Line

Money follows the full file: grades clear the bar, evidence proves trajectory, and the narrative explains why funding you is a good investment. Your CGPA's job is to keep you in the room; the rest of your application wins it.
