If you finished Class 10 in a CBSE school before 2018, your certificate shows a five-point scale of grade points — a CGPA — instead of a percentage. Years later, job and university forms still ask you to convert it. This guide explains exactly how that system worked and how to convert your figure correctly.

## The CBSE Grading System (2010–2017)

During these years, CBSE reported Class 10 results as **five subject-wise grade points** on a 10-point scale, plus an overall CGPA (their simple average). The mapping:

| Grade | Marks Band | Grade Point |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | 91–100 | 10 |
| A2 | 81–90 | 9 |
| B1 | 71–80 | 8 |
| B2 | 61–70 | 7 |
| C1 | 51–60 | 6 |
| C2 | 41–50 | 5 |
| D | 33–40 | 4 |
| E1 / E2 | Below 33 | Fail |

Your overall CGPA was the average of your five subject grade points. For example, grades of 10, 9, 9, 8, 8 average to a CGPA of **8.8**.

## The ×9.5 Formula — and Where 9.5 Came From

CBSE's official conversion is:

```
Percentage = CGPA × 9.5
```

The multiplier wasn't arbitrary. CBSE analysed the marks of students who scored a perfect 10 grade point and found their average marks were about 95. Since 10 grade points corresponded to 95 marks, one grade point was worth 9.5 percentage points — hence the formula. An 8.8 CGPA converts to 8.8 × 9.5 = **83.6%**.

## Why CBSE Dropped the System

From 2018, CBSE returned to marks-based results for Class 10, citing feedback that grade bands obscured meaningful differences between students (an 81 and a 90 both showed as A2) and complicated admissions. Your old certificate, however, remains valid — and its CGPA remains the official representation of your Class 10 performance forever.

## Using Your Old CGPA Today

- **Job applications asking for 10th percentage:** convert with ×9.5 and write the result. This is the CBSE-prescribed figure, and HR teams that verify against CBSE records expect exactly it.
- **Forms asking for "marks obtained":** where the form truly requires marks, use the converted percentage; where it allows CGPA, write "CGPA 8.8/10 (CBSE)".
- **Don't re-derive from your subject marks.** If you remember your raw marks, resist the urge to average them — the certificate's CGPA is the canonical record, and the ×9.5 conversion of *that* number is the defensible percentage.

## A Note for Current Students

Modern CBSE results (Class 10 and 12) report marks directly, so no conversion is needed. The ×9.5 formula now survives mainly in two places: conversions of pre-2018 Class 10 certificates, and the **UGC's default convention for universities that haven't published their own rule** — which is why the multiplier appears on so many Indian calculators, including our own [conversion chart](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) covering SPPU, VTU, Mumbai University, Anna University, AKTU, GTU and KTU.
