Your resume has perhaps six seconds to make an impression, and the education section raises more formatting questions than any other. Should you write CGPA, percentage, or both? Out of 10 or as GPA? What if your CGPA is unimpressive? Here are the conventions that actually matter, with examples.

## When to Include Your CGPA

**Include it when:**

- You are a student or recent graduate (roughly your first 1–3 years) — recruiters expect it.
- The job posting mentions an eligibility criterion (e.g., "7.0 CGPA or above") — meeting it visibly helps you pass screens.
- Your CGPA is genuinely strong (roughly 7.5+/10 or 3.0+/4.0).

**Consider omitting it when:**

- You have 3+ years of work experience — your track record has replaced it.
- Your CGPA is below the common screening bar (under ~6.0) and the application doesn't demand it. An omission is neutral; a weak number invites comparison.

Never inflate or round up aggressively (writing 8 when your transcript says 7.6). Background verification is routine at Indian IT services and universal at product companies — a mismatch here can revoke an offer after you've accepted it.

## The Standard Formats

**Indian resumes (10-point scale):**

```
B.E. Computer Engineering, Savitribai Phule Pune University — 2026
CGPA: 8.42/10 (76.7%)
```

**If the posting asks for percentage:**

```
B.Tech Mechanical Engineering, VTU — 2025
Percentage: 84.2% (CGPA 8.42/10)
```

**International applications (4.0 scale):**

```
B.Tech Computer Science, Anna University, India — 2025
CGPA: 8.6/10.0 (≈ 3.44/4.0)
```

Note the pattern: **one primary number with the scale stated, plus the equivalent in brackets.** The scale ("/10") is what makes the number meaningful; the equivalent saves the reader a conversion step and shows you know your own record. Convert the equivalent with your university's official formula — our [university calculators](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) give you the exact figure, and the [CGPA to GPA tool](/cgpa-to-gpa) handles 4.0-scale conversions.

## Placement Rules Within the Education Section

1. **Reverse chronological order.** Highest or most recent qualification first: PG, then UG, then 12th, then 10th.
2. **Consistency across entries.** If you show CGPA for your degree, show percentages (boards report percentages) for 10th/12th with their own values — don't force-convert board results into CGPA.
3. **Include the university name in full.** "SPPU" alone forces the reader to guess; "Savitribai Phule Pune University" is searchable and verifiable.
4. **Add the year of graduation.** Many eligibility filters are year-based (campus programs, fresher drives).
5. **Skip the school name for 10th/12th** unless space allows — board, year and percentage are what screens check.

## Special Situations

**"First Class with Distinction"** can be added in brackets if it strengthens a government or PSU application where classification matters — but only if your university's ordinance actually awards it (see [division rules](/guides/first-class-second-class-division)).

**Improving trajectory:** if your final-year CGPA is much better than your aggregate, you may write "Final Year CGPA: 8.8/10" as a secondary line. It's honest, and trajectory reads well — but always keep the aggregate visible too.

**Backlogs:** never mention cleared backlogs on a resume. If asked in interviews, answer honestly and briefly; the resume is not the place.

**Rank information:** if your university provides rank or percentile ("Department Rank 3/120"), that can be more persuasive than CGPA — include it alongside, not instead.

## A Complete Education Section Example

```
EDUCATION

M.Tech Data Science, IIT Hyderabad — 2027 (expected)
  CGPA: 8.9/10

B.E. Computer Engineering, Pune University — 2025
  CGPA: 8.42/10 (76.7%) · First Class with Distinction

Class XII (CBSE), Delhi — 2021
  91.2%

Class X (CBSE), Delhi — 2019
  93.4% (CGPA 9.4/10)
```

Clean, verifiable, consistent — exactly what a six-second scan is looking for.
