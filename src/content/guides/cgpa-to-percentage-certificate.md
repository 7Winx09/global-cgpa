Sooner or later, an official form will refuse to accept a self-calculated percentage. Visa authorities, PSU verifiers and some foreign universities want the conversion *on your university's letterhead* — that document is the conversion certificate. Here's what it is, when you need it, and how to get one without the usual runaround.

## What the Certificate Is

A **CGPA-to-percentage conversion certificate** (sometimes called an equivalence certificate) is an official document from your university stating:

- Your name, roll/registration number and programme
- Your final CGPA
- The equivalent percentage, computed with the university's prescribed formula
- Usually, the formula itself and the ordinance reference

Because it carries the university's seal and signature, it converts your CGPA with *authority* — no verifier can second-guess the figure the way they might question your own multiplication.

## When You Actually Need One

- **PSU and government recruitment** — many public sector undertakings ask for certified conversion during document verification.
- **Visa and immigration** — some countries' credential assessments want university-certified percentages.
- **Foreign university admissions** — occasionally requested alongside transcripts, especially for direct PhD applications.
- **Scholarship verification** — national and international schemes sometimes require certified figures.
- **Legal or court documentation** — any context where your academic record becomes evidence.

For everyday purposes (job portals, most private employers, most forms), a self-converted figure using your university's official formula is accepted. Get the certificate when something *official* demands it.

## How to Apply: The General Process

While each university has its own portal, the pattern is consistent:

1. **Get the application form** — from your college's examination cell, the university exam section, or the student portal.
2. **Attach documents** — typically your consolidated marksheet (photocopy), degree certificate or provisional certificate, and ID proof.
3. **Pay the fee** — usually ₹100–₹1,000 depending on the university and speed.
4. **Collect in 1–4 weeks** — either from the exam section or by post. Ask for 2–3 copies; reissues cost more than extras.

## University-Specific Notes

- **SPPU:** apply through your college exam cell or the university's online student services; engineering students receive the conversion per the (CGPA × 10) − 7.5 ordinance.
- **VTU:** the ×10 conversion is printed in VTU circulars; certificates issue through the regional VTU centres or your college's principal's office.
- **Mumbai University:** the Kalina campus exam section handles conversions under the 7.1×CGPA + 11 rule; many affiliated colleges forward applications in batches.
- **Anna University:** request through your college's COE; the ×10 rule from the regulations is applied.
- **AKTU:** conversion certificates are available through the university's student service portal and exam cells, using the (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 ordinance formula.
- **GTU:** the Student Services Portal handles academic services including conversion certificates under Notification 1/2012's formula.
- **KTU:** apply through your college or the university's e-Gov portal; certificates issued after the 2023 revision use CGPA × 10.

## Pro Tips That Save Weeks

- **Apply during non-result periods.** Exam-section queues explode when semester results drop; off-peak applications turn around in days.
- **Ask for multiple originals.** Verifiers often keep copies; a second original costs a fraction of a fresh application.
- **Scan and archive.** A clear PDF of the certificate means you can meet digital deadlines instantly and courier originals later.
- **Check the formula on the certificate.** Errors happen — if the percentage doesn't match your university's ordinance, return it for correction immediately rather than discovering the mismatch at a verification desk.
- **For abroad use, ask about transcripts too.** Many foreign evaluators (WES and similar) want semester-wise transcripts *in addition to* the conversion; order both in one trip.
