Every student planning an MS abroad eventually faces an application form with a field marked "GPA (4.0 scale)" and an Indian transcript that says 8.6/10. What you write in that field — and what you *don't* — matters more than most guides admit. Here's how to handle it properly.

## The Quick Conversion (For Self-Assessment)

The standard linear conversion rescales the 10-point CGPA to 4.0:

```
GPA (4.0) = (CGPA ÷ 10) × 4
```

| CGPA (India) | Linear GPA (4.0) |
|---|---|
| 9.5 | 3.80 |
| 9.0 | 3.60 |
| 8.5 | 3.40 |
| 8.0 | 3.20 |
| 7.5 | 3.00 |
| 7.0 | 2.80 |
| 6.5 | 2.60 |

## The Part Most Guides Get Wrong

Here's the counterintuitive truth: **most US universities do not want you to self-convert.** Application systems ask for your cumulative average *and its scale* precisely so their admissions teams can interpret it. What actually happens to your number:

1. **You enter your original CGPA with the scale stated:** "8.6/10.0".
2. **International credentials go through an evaluator** — WES (World Education Services), ECE, or whichever service your target university specifies.
3. **The evaluator converts course-by-course**, using methodology calibrated to Indian grading (accounting for the fact that a 70 in an Indian engineering programme represents different learning than a 70 in a US course).

The result frequently differs from the linear figure — sometimes higher, sometimes lower. A WES evaluation of an 8.2 CGPA might land anywhere from 3.2 to 3.6 depending on your institution's grading culture and your course mix.

## What to Actually Do, Step by Step

1. **Enter your original CGPA wherever the form allows it.** "8.6/10.0" is a complete, honest answer.
2. **If a 4.0 figure is mandatory** (some forms hard-require it), use the linear conversion and label it: "3.44/4.0 (linear conversion from 8.6/10.0)".
3. **Order the required evaluation early.** WES course-by-course evaluations take weeks; start when you begin applications, not the week of the deadline.
4. **Never inflate.** Self-reporting a rounded-up GPA that your official evaluation contradicts can get an admit revoked. Evaluators' numbers are the ones universities trust.
5. **Keep your transcripts consistent** — the CGPA on your application must match your consolidated marksheet exactly, including decimals.

## What CGPA Do You Need for an MS Abroad?

- **Safe zone for most programmes:** roughly 8.0+ CGPA (≈3.2+ linear) — competitive for most ranked US programmes, and strong for Canada, UK and Australia.
- **Minimum viable:** around 7.0–7.5 (2.8–3.0) clears many programmes' posted minimums, especially outside the top tier.
- **Below 7.0:** still possible — strong GRE/GATE scores, research experience, publications and work experience routinely compensate, and some European universities care more about subject grades than the cumulative figure.
- **Germany:** many public universities convert your grade to the *Bavarian formula* rather than the 4.0 scale — a different calculation entirely, usually handled by uni-assist.

## Country Notes

- **US/Canada:** WES-style evaluation culture; original scale plus evaluation is the norm.
- **UK:** most universities map your percentage/CGPA to their own first-class/upper-second equivalents; they publish conversion tables per Indian institution.
- **Australia:** similar to the UK — institutional conversion tables, usually generous.
- **Germany:** Bavarian formula; your grade matters less than module-level relevance to the programme.

## The One-Paragraph Summary

Report your CGPA as-is with its scale, use the linear 4.0 figure only when a form forces the issue, let WES or an equivalent evaluator produce the official conversion, and spend the time you saved on the parts of your application that actually differentiate you — statement of purpose, projects, and recommendations.
