Every placement season starts with the same anxiety: *is my CGPA good enough?* The honest answer depends on which companies you're targeting. This guide lays out the actual cut-off bands companies use, how the filters work, and your options if your CGPA is already locked in below your target.

## The Short Answer

For Indian campus placements:

- **6.0 – 6.5 CGPA** clears most mass-service recruiters
- **7.0 – 7.5 CGPA** clears nearly all service companies and many mid-tier product roles
- **8.0+ CGPA** is competitive for top product companies, quant firms and core R&D roles
- **Below 6.0** shrinks your pool sharply, but does not close it

Remember these are CGPA values on a 10-point scale. If a company states its cut-off in percentage, convert carefully — 60% at your university may not be 6.3 CGPA. Our [university calculators](/cgpa-to-percentage-chart) handle the exact conversion.

## Cut-offs by Company Type

| Segment | Typical cut-off | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Mass IT services | 6.0–6.5 CGPA, often 60% | TCS (NQT), Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini, Accenture |
| Mid-tier services & consulting | 6.5–7.5 CGPA | LTIMindtree, Persistent, ZS Associates (higher bands) |
| Product / tech companies | 7.0–8.0+ CGPA | Microsoft, Adobe, Oracle, Salesforce, Flipkart |
| Top-tier tech & quant | 8.0+ CGPA (soft filter) | Google, Amazon, Atlassian, DE-shaw, Tower Research |
| Core engineering & PSUs | 60–65% or 6.5–7.0 CGPA | L&T, Tata Motors, Bosch, plus PSU GATE-based recruitment |
| Startups | Rarely filter on CGPA | Skills and demos dominate |

Two nuances matter:

1. **The published cut-off is a floor, not the bar.** When 2,000 students clear a 6.0 cut-off for 50 seats, shortlisting effectively happens at 7.5+. Cut-offs clear you for consideration; they don't win interviews.
2. **"No active backlogs" is a separate filter.** Many companies require a clean record at the time of joining, regardless of CGPA.

## How Companies Actually Apply the Filter

- **Percentage vs CGPA:** application portals often ask for both, or auto-convert with a fixed formula. If the portal's conversion differs from your university's, enter what your documents show — consistency is what verification checks.
- **Aggregate across 10th, 12th and degree:** several service companies require 60% *in each* of 10th, 12th and graduation, not just the degree.
- **First-attempt conditions:** a few companies (and most PSUs via GATE scoring) add "no gaps / no backlogs" clauses. A cleared backlog usually passes; an active one usually doesn't.

## If Your CGPA Is Below Your Target

Your CGPA's leverage depends on how many credits are still in play. Use the [SGPA to CGPA calculator](/sgpa-to-cgpa) to model what strong final semesters do to your average — with 60+ credits remaining, a 9.0-level final year can lift a 6.8 to around 7.3.

If the number is effectively locked:

1. **Target segments that don't filter hard** — startups, and services hiring in bulk during demand spikes.
2. **Build proof-of-work:** shipped projects, open-source contributions, internships. Off-campus product hiring weighs these far more than on-campus filters do.
3. **Use referrals to bypass resume screens.** A referral skips the CGPA filter at many companies because a human vouches for you first.
4. **Score well in their tests.** A strong coding round result routinely overrides a mediocre CGPA at product companies.
5. **Consider a master's degree.** A strong GATE score or a good MS application (see [CGPA for studying abroad](/guides/cgpa-to-gpa-4-scale-ms-abroad)) resets the board.

## The One-Line Truth

A high CGPA buys you *at-bats* — more companies willing to test you. Beyond roughly 7.5, returns diminish sharply, and skills take over entirely. Below it, the game is about maximising at-bats through off-campus applications, referrals and demonstrable work. Neither situation is a verdict; they're just different strategies.
